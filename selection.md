# Selection — Funniest Time Machine Warning Label

Gate: one line, 1–120 characters, genuine warning-label tone.

## Candidates (scored 1–5 against the judge brief, in its order)

1. "WARNING: Do not meet yourself. He knows all your excuses and he has had years to practice them." — 95 chars. Hazard (duplicate self) IS the joke; surprise lands in the last four words; straight official face. **WINNER**
2. "WARNING: If you meet yourself, do not argue. You have never won an argument against you." — sharp, but the twist ("against you") is a wordplay turn rather than a hazard-driven joke; loses to #1 on criterion 2.
3. "CAUTION: Time travel may strand you in the past. Your ancestors have already filed a complaint." — ancestor hazard + bureaucratic twist, but "filed a complaint" is the joke, not the stranding itself; loses to #1 on criterion 2.
4. "WARNING: Do not step on butterflies. One operator did; he now owes rent to his own grandfather." — vivid butterfly effect, but the punchline is a story, not the hazard turning on the reader; loses to #1 on criterion 3 (surprise is mid-sentence, not the last words).
5. "WARNING: Paradoxes are not covered by warranty. Neither is the version of you reading this." — big surprise, but it names the hazard ("Paradoxes") flatly in the opening clause, which criterion 2 penalises; loses to #1.
6. "NOTICE: The machine returns you to the moment you left. Your problems will still be there, older." — "older" is a decent twist, but the hazard is only implied and the joke is mild; loses on criterion 2.
7. "WARNING: Meeting your past self voids the warranty. He will also insist on driving." — cute, but "insist on driving" is a random quirk, not the hazard biting; loses on criterion 2.
8. "CAUTION: Do not leave the machine running. The past has a habit of following you home." — atmospheric, not a laugh; loses on criterion 2.
9. "WARNING: You cannot change the past. You can, however, embarrass yourself in front of it." — names the rule flatly ("cannot change the past"), then jokes around it; loses to #1 on criterion 2.
10. "WARNING: If you see yourself, walk away. He is not lost; he is avoiding you on purpose." — funny premise, but "avoiding you on purpose" undercuts the hazard instead of turning it; loses to #1 on criterion 2.

## Duel summary

#1 beats #5 on criterion 2 (hazard turned into joke vs hazard named flatly), beats #4 on criterion 3 (surprise in the last words), and beats #2 on criterion 2 (the duplicate self's practiced excuses are the hazard itself, not a wordplay). No other candidate wins a criterion against #1. The field-study bar for this format is a straight-faced notice whose punchline reframes the hazard on the reader — #1 is the only candidate that does both in under 100 characters.

## Winner

"WARNING: Do not meet yourself. He knows all your excuses and he has had years to practice them." (95 characters, emitter-verified)
